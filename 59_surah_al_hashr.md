```
Structural Outline
I. The Power of Allah in the Exile of Banu Nadir (Verses 1–5)

Theme: Allah's authority over history and nations.

Universal glorification of Allah (v.1)
The expulsion of the Banu Nadir from Madinah
Believers learn that victory comes from Allah, not military strength
The destruction of palm trees occurred by Allah's permission
Demonstrates Allah's wisdom in warfare and judgment

Key idea: Allah fulfills His decree even when events seem impossible.

II. Distribution of War Gains (Fay') and Social Justice (Verses 6–10)

Theme: Economic justice and community building.

A. Ownership of Fay' (vv.6–7)
Property gained without battle belongs to Allah and His Messenger.
It is distributed among:
the poor,
relatives,
orphans,
travelers,
those in need.

Purpose:

"So that wealth does not circulate only among the rich."

B. Three Ideal Groups of Believers

1. The Muhajirun (v.8)

Left their homes for Allah.
Described as truthful.

2. The Ansar (v.9)

Love the emigrants.
Prefer others over themselves.
Free from greed.

3. Later believers (v.10)

Pray for previous believers.
Ask Allah to remove resentment from their hearts.

Key idea: A healthy Muslim community is built on generosity, sacrifice, and love.

III. The Hypocrites and Their False Promises (Verses 11–17)

Theme: The danger of hypocrisy.

Hypocrites secretly support the Jewish tribe.
Promise military help.
They abandon them when conflict begins.
Compared to Satan:
Satan invites disbelief.
Then abandons the person afterward.

Lesson:
Do not trust alliances built on hypocrisy.

IV. A Call to Self-Reflection (Verses 18–21)

Theme: Preparing for the Hereafter.

Verse 18

Every soul should examine what it has prepared for tomorrow.

Verses 19–20

Do not become like those who forgot Allah.

Contrast:

People of Hell
People of Paradise
Verse 21

The famous mountain example:

If the Qur'an had been revealed upon a mountain,
it would humble itself and split from fear of Allah.

Key idea: Human hearts should be softer than mountains.

V. The Majesty of Allah (Verses 22–24)

Theme: Allah's Beautiful Names.

The surah concludes with a powerful description of Allah.

Examples include:

Ar-Rahman (The Most Merciful)
Ar-Rahim (The Especially Merciful)
Al-Malik (The King)
Al-Quddus (The Most Holy)
As-Salam (The Source of Peace)
Al-Mu'min (The Granter of Security)
Al-Muhaymin (The Guardian)
Al-Aziz (The Almighty)
Al-Hakim (The All-Wise)

The surah ends by emphasizing that everything in the heavens and the earth glorifies Allah.

Chiastic (Literary) Structure

Many scholars observe a balanced literary pattern:

Section	Verses	Theme
A	1	All creation glorifies Allah
B	2–5	Allah's judgment on Banu Nadir
C	6–10	Building the righteous community through justice and generosity
D	11–17	The hypocrisy of false allies (central warning)
C′	18–20	Building the righteous community through self-accountability
B′	21	Allah's overwhelming majesty illustrated by the mountain
A′	22–24	All creation glorifies Allah through His Beautiful Names
```
